GTA 6: A guide for families

A plain-language guide to GTA 6 for parents, buyers, and the people who live with them. No game required.

Last updated: November 22, 2026. Ratings, prices, and sizes can change, so confirm on your console's store page before you buy.

Launch scam checker

Big launches attract scammers. Tick anything that matches the offer, link, or message you're looking at. This checker runs entirely in your browser and sends nothing anywhere.

The simplest rule: the game is sold through your console's own store (PlayStation Store or Microsoft Store/Xbox) and major retailers. Anything that sends you somewhere else for a "key," "beta," or "free copy" deserves suspicion.

If you already clicked or paid

  • Change the password for any account you typed in, and turn on two-step verification. Change it anywhere you reused that password.
  • If you paid, contact your bank or card company right away and ask about a chargeback.
  • If you installed something, run a security scan, and consider a factory reset for a phone.
  • Report the scam at reportfraud.ftc.gov (US) and to the platform where you found it.

Parent's guide

The short version: GTA 6 is rated M (Mature 17+) by the ESRB in the US and PEGI 18 in Europe. It is an adult game.

What the rating says

The ESRB lists these content descriptors:

Blood and GoreIntense ViolenceMature HumorNudityStrong LanguageStrong Sexual ContentUse of Drugs and Alcohol

It also carries an In-Game Purchases notice, which means money can be spent inside the game or its services. Ratings and wording vary by country, so read the descriptors on your own store listing. The descriptors tell you what kinds of content are in the game. They don't say how often it appears or whether a player can avoid it.

If your child asks for it

  • Read the full ESRB entry for the game at esrb.org before deciding.
  • Ask what draws them to it. Often it's friends playing, a clip they saw, or the hype.
  • Decide as a household. "Not yet" is a complete answer, and you can revisit it.
  • Check where else they might play it: a friend's house, a cousin's console, a streamed video.

Set limits on the console

Both consoles let a parent control which games a child's account can play and how much they can spend. Menu names change with system updates. If you don't see these exactly, search "parental controls" or "family" in your console's settings or support site.

PlayStation 5
  • Create a child account under Family Management, so the child plays on their own login rather than yours.
  • Go to Settings > Family and Parental Controls > Family Management, select the child, then Parental Controls. Set the age level for games and apps so mature-rated games are blocked.
  • Under Parental Controls, set a Monthly Spending Limit, which can be zero, to cover in-game purchases.
  • Keep your own adult account's password private and don't leave it signed in.
Xbox Series X|S
  • Add your child to your Microsoft family group, so they have their own child account.
  • In Microsoft's family settings (the Xbox Family Settings app or your Microsoft account's family page), set the age limit for apps and games.
  • Turn on Ask to Buy in the Xbox Family Settings app for requests to buy things beyond the child's available balance. Control how much money you add; purchases covered by that balance may not need another approval.
  • Keep your adult account's sign-in private.
One caution: an adult account that is signed in on a shared console can bypass child settings. Sign out when you're done.

Talking points

  • Explain that the rating is about content, in the same way a movie's R rating is.
  • Agree on screen-time and spending rules before a new game arrives, not after.
  • Remind them never to share their login, and never to download "free" versions of the game.

GTA 6 for the rest of us

Someone in your life is playing it, and you'd like to follow the conversation. Here's the spoiler-free version.

What it is

It's the newest game in Rockstar Games' Grand Theft Auto series, a crime story set in a big open world you can roam freely. It's rated Mature 17+, so it's built for adults.

Why everyone is excited

  • It came out on November 19, 2026 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.
  • It's the first numbered entry in the series in a very long time, so the wait was long.
  • The release date moved more than once. It was first slated for May 2025, then May 2026, and finally November 2026.

Words you'll hear

Open world
A huge map you can explore in any order, with no fixed path.
Story mode / campaign
The main storyline, played alone. Many players start here.
Preload
Downloading the game before launch day so it's ready at midnight.
Patch
A software update that fixes problems after release.
Heist
A planned robbery. A staple of the series.
Sandbox
A game with room to mess around and make your own fun.

How to be a good sport

  • Ask them what they're up to. Players love to be asked, and a single question can buy you ten minutes of delighted explanation.
  • Don't ask for spoilers or give any. Let them find the story themselves.
  • Expect long sessions at first. A game this big has a way of eating the weekend.
  • If it's a kid asking to play, jump to the Parent's guide.
